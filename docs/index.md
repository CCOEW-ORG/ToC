## About This Course

Theory of Computation (ToC) sits at the heart of computer science. It asks — and answers — the most fundamental questions about what computers *can* and *cannot* do:

- What does it mean to *compute* something?
- What problems can be solved mechanically, and which ones are forever beyond the reach of any machine?
- How do you characterize the *difficulty* of a problem? Can two very different-looking problems be shown to be fundamentally the same?
- What are the minimal resources (memory, time) needed to solve a class of problems?

This course builds a rigorous mathematical framework to answer these questions, moving from the simplest possible machines (Finite Automata) up through increasingly powerful models (Pushdown Automata, Turing Machines).

## Course Objectives

By the end of this course you will be able to:

1. Recall and apply the mathematical concepts underpinning formal languages and machines.
2. Design computational models — Finite Automata, Regular Expressions, Context-Free Grammars — for given languages.
3. Convert between equivalent representations of a language (e.g. NFA ↔ DFA, Regex ↔ NFA, Grammar ↔ FA).
4. Design Pushdown Automata and Turing Machines for languages beyond the reach of finite automata.
